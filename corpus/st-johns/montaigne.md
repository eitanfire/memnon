## Passage: To Philosophize Is to Learn to Die
Let us learn to stand firm, and to fight death. And to begin to deprive death of the greatest advantage it has over us, let us take a way entirely different from the usual one. Let us deprive death of its strangeness, let us frequent it, let us be used to it; let us have nothing so often in mind as death.

*Essays, "That to Philosophize Is to Learn to Die"*

## Passage: What Do I Know?
When I play with my cat, who knows if I am not a pastime to her more than she is to me?

*Essays, "Apology for Raymond Sebond"*

## Passage: The Self as Subject
Every man carries the whole stamp of the human condition within him. Authors communicate with the people by some special extrinsic mark; I am the first to do so by my entire being, as Michel de Montaigne, not as a grammarian or a poet or a jurist.

*Essays, Book III, Chapter 2, "Of Repentance"*

## Passage: On Friendship
Because it was him, because it was me. In friendship there is no other explanation.

*Essays, "On Friendship" (on his friendship with La Boétie)*

## Passage: We Are Never at Home
We are never at home, we are always beyond ourselves. Fear, desire, hope push us toward the future and steal from us the feeling and consideration of what is, to amuse us with what will be, even when we shall be no more.

*Essays, "That the Taste of Good and Evil Depends in Large Part on the Opinion We Have of Them"*

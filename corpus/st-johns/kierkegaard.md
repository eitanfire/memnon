## Passage: The Either/Or
It is perfectly true, as philosophers say, that life can only be understood backwards. But they forget the other proposition, that it must be lived forwards.

*Kierkegaard, Journals (1843)*

## Passage: The Stages of Existence
There are three stages on life's way: the aesthetic stage, where one lives for pleasure and immediate experience; the ethical stage, where one lives by duty and universal law; and the religious stage, where one stands alone before God in fear and trembling.

*Kierkegaard, Stages on Life's Way (paraphrase)*

## Passage: The Leap of Faith
Without risk, there is no faith. Faith is precisely the contradiction between the infinite passion of the individual's inwardness and the objective uncertainty.

*Kierkegaard, Concluding Unscientific Postscript, p. 182*

## Passage: Anxiety Is the Dizziness of Freedom
Anxiety is the dizziness of freedom. Anxiety arises as freedom looks down into its own possibility, and then reaches out to grasp at finitude to steady itself — and in that grasping, falls.

*Kierkegaard, The Concept of Anxiety, Chapter I (paraphrase)*

## Passage: The Self as Task
The self is a relation that relates itself to itself. The human being is spirit. But what is spirit? Spirit is the self. But what is the self? The self is a relation which relates itself to its own self.

*Kierkegaard, The Sickness Unto Death, Part One, A*

## Passage: Despair as the Failure to Be Oneself
The most common form of despair is not being who you are.

*Kierkegaard, The Sickness Unto Death (paraphrase)*

## Passage: Abraham and the Absurd (Fear and Trembling)
Abraham had faith, and had faith for this life. Had his faith been only for a life to come, he would more easily have cast aside all that he held dear in this world. But Abraham's faith was not of this abstract kind. He believed that God would not require Isaac of him, and yet was ready if required. He climbed the mountain by virtue of the absurd.

*Kierkegaard, Fear and Trembling, Problema I (paraphrase)*

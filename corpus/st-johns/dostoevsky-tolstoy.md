## Passage: The Grand Inquisitor — Freedom and Bread
Man is tormented by no greater anxiety than to find someone quickly to whom he can hand over that gift of freedom with which the ill-fated creature is born.

*Dostoevsky, The Brothers Karamazov, Book V, Chapter 5*

## Passage: Love in Practice
Love in action is a harsh and dreadful thing compared with love in dreams. Love in dreams thirsts for immediate action, rapidly performed and in the sight of all. Men will even give their lives for it. But active love is labor and fortitude.

*Dostoevsky, The Brothers Karamazov, Father Zosima*

## Passage: Beauty Will Save the World
Beauty will save the world.

*Dostoevsky, The Idiot, Part III, Chapter 5 (Prince Myshkin)*

## Passage: The Death of Ivan Ilyich — What Had Been Wrong
What had formerly seemed just — the Decrees, the service, the arrangements of his life, his family and the social and official interests of these people — all that seemed to him now not the real thing at all. He suddenly understood that it was all not so, all a dreadful, gigantic deception that concealed both life and death.

*Tolstoy, The Death of Ivan Ilyich, Chapter IX*

## Passage: The Simple Thing
In place of death there was light. "So that's it!" he exclaimed. "What joy!"

*Tolstoy, The Death of Ivan Ilyich, Chapter XII*

## Passage: Pierre's Conversion (War and Peace)
He did not see the great, the eternal, and the infinite in anything; he only saw his garden, the oak trees, his own life. And in this personal, private life was the whole significance of life for him now.

*Tolstoy, War and Peace, Epilogue (paraphrase of Pierre's transformation)*

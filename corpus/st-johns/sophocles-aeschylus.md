## Passage: The Tyranny of Not Knowing
Oedipus: I must pursue this trail to the end, till I have unravelled the mystery of my birth.
Jocasta: No! Listen to me, I beg you! Don't do it!
Oedipus: I will not listen. I must know the truth.

*Sophocles, Oedipus Rex (paraphrase of 1058–1075)*

## Passage: Tiresias to Oedipus
It is not fate that I should be your ruin, Apollo is enough; it is his care to work this out.
You have your eyes but see not where you are in sin, nor where you live, nor whom you live with.

*Sophocles, Oedipus Rex, 376–379 (trans. Grene)*

## Passage: Sight and Blindness
Oedipus: What a dreadful waking from a dream! To have known such happiness, and now to have it all turn black.

*Sophocles, Oedipus Rex*

## Passage: Unwritten Law (Antigone)
I did not think your edicts were so strong that they could overrule the gods' unwritten and unfailing laws. Not now, nor yesterday's, they always live, and no one knows their origin in time.

*Sophocles, Antigone, 450–457 (trans. Grene)*

## Passage: The Chorus on Human Daring (Antigone)
Many are the wonders of the world, and none more wonderful than man. Over the grey sea in the stormy south wind he goes, making his path through the waves that tower above him. And Earth, most ancient and inexhaustible, he wears away, turning the soil with horse and plough.

*Sophocles, Antigone, first choral ode (paraphrase)*

## Passage: Zeus and Justice (Aeschylus)
Zeus, who guided mortals to be wise, has established his law: wisdom comes through suffering. Drop by drop the pain that reminds us of our wounds falls on the heart in sleep, and even against our will, comes wisdom to us.

*Aeschylus, Agamemnon, 177–183 (trans. Lattimore)*

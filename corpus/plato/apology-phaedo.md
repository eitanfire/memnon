## Passage: The Examined Life
The unexamined life is not worth living.

*Apology, 38a*

## Passage: What Socrates Fears
I know that I know nothing. And that is the beginning of wisdom.

*Apology (paraphrase of 21d)*

## Passage: Facing Death
No one knows whether death may not be the greatest of all blessings for a man, yet men fear it as if they knew that it is the greatest of evils.

*Apology, 29a*

## Passage: The Mission
I spend all my time going about trying to persuade you, young and old, to make your first and chief concern not for your bodies or your possessions, but for the highest welfare of your souls.

*Apology, 30a–b*

## Passage: The Philosopher's Task
True philosophers make dying their profession, and to them of all men death is least alarming.

*Phaedo, 67e*

## Passage: On the Soul's Immortality
The soul takes nothing with it to the other world but its education and culture; and these, it is said, are of the greatest service or of the greatest injury to the dead man, at the very beginning of his journey thither.

*Phaedo, 107d*

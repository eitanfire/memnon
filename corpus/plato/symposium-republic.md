## Passage: The Ladder of Beauty (Diotima)
He who has been instructed thus far in the things of love, and who has learned to see the beautiful in due order and succession, when he comes toward the end will suddenly perceive a nature of wondrous beauty — and this, Socrates, is the final cause of all our former toils.

*Symposium, 210e–211a*

## Passage: Love as Longing
Love is the desire for the perpetual possession of the good.

*Symposium, 206a*

## Passage: The Allegory of the Cave
Imagine human beings living in an underground dwelling, like a cave. They have been there since childhood, with their legs and necks chained so that they cannot move and can only look at the wall in front of them. The shadows they see are the only reality they know.

*Republic, 514a–b*

## Passage: The Return to the Cave
Those who have been released from the cave and have seen the light must return to it, and take their turn at the labor of politics and the guidance of others.

*Republic, 519c–d*

## Passage: Justice in the Soul
Justice is doing one's own work and not meddling with what isn't one's own. And injustice is the opposite — it is a kind of civil war in the soul.

*Republic, 433a, 444b*

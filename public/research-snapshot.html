What teachers told me, and what I'm building next

Memnon started as a voice-reflection workflow I built for myself. Before turning it into a product for teachers, I went looking for the people closest to the problem. This page shows what they told me, how it changed the product, and where it goes next.

🔒 Everyone here is anonymized: roles only, no names, no organizations, and no one's own words. Memnon keeps teachers' reflections in their own Google Drive, so none of that appears here either.

How I got here

The research came out of a product that didn't stick.

The first bet

Teach League

An AI app for planning lessons and grading. Teachers liked it when I showed it to them, but very few kept using it on their own.

Why it didn't stick

One more system

New teachers needed help with planning but were already juggling too many new tools. Seasoned teachers had their own systems. What both called hardest was the never-ending demands and criticism.

The new hypothesis

Reflection, not productivity

Maybe what teachers need is a place to process a hard day out loud, the way they would with a trusted colleague. I took that question to InnEdCO.

What came up across speakers

Themes shared by the speakers on a panel about teaching citizenship in the age of AI, plus two sessions that shaped the product.

How to support teachers without overwhelming them
Institutional adoption versus what works in a real classroom
What responsible AI looks like in actual implementation
Citizenship, ethics and truth when students use AI
AI literacy versus digital literacy

Session: scaffolding new teachers

New teachers often ask for advice but still need concrete language and moves they can use in the moment. Parent communication templates and non-punitive observation were framed as retention support, not compliance.

Session: AI citizenship, process over product

If students are graded only on right answers, they'll take the fastest path. Teachers were already using AI for reflection and spoken responses that value explanation and process over speed.

What it changed

Each decision traces back to something I heard.

What I heardThe most urgent need was support for new teachers, from the people who mentor them.
What I didBuilt Memnon for teachers in mentored programs, not as a general productivity tool.
What I heardHonest reflection only happens when it feels safe and private.
What I didMade reflection private by default: each teacher's reflections live in their own Google Drive, not on Memnon's servers.
What I heardTeachers are already overwhelmed by new systems.
What I didDelivered the result where they already are: a short daily podcast in the podcast app they already use.
What I heardGeneric AI advice feels like a sales pitch, not like real teaching.
What I didGrounded every reflection in subject- and grade-specific practice and in thinkers each teacher chooses, from bell hooks to John Dewey.

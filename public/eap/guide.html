Making the most of your EAP sessions

Six sessions go fast

Most school-district employee assistance programs (EAPs) cover a short run of free, confidential counseling sessions, usually 3 to 8. Your district pays for them and your employer does not learn that you used them. Most programs also cover the people in your household.

Six sessions is not much time. Here is how teachers get more out of it.

Find your Colorado district's EAP

Before the first session

  • Call the number, not just the website. The intake call is where you get matched. Say you're a teacher, and say whether you want evenings, video or in person.
  • Ask how sessions are counted. Some districts count "per episode" (per issue) and some "per year." The answer tells you whether you can come back later for something new.
  • Write down three things. What is hardest right now, what you have already tried, and what "better" would look like by session six.

During sessions

  • Ask for homework. A short practice between sessions helps the time in the room carry over.
  • Steer gently. If a session drifts, it's fine to say, "Can we come back to the thing I wanted to work on?" Your time is limited and it belongs to you.
  • Ask what's next at session four. That leaves time to plan: a referral, continuing on your insurance, or tools to keep going alone.

Recording a session (only with your therapist's agreement)

Some people find it helps to record sessions and listen again, or to have a tool summarize them. If you want to:

  • Ask first. Always. Even where the law allows a participant to record, recording without asking can damage the trust therapy depends on. A simple ask: "Would you be comfortable if I recorded our sessions for my own reflection? It stays private to me, and I can delete it any time."
  • Know the law where both of you are. Colorado lets a person record a conversation they are part of. Video sessions often cross state lines, though, and some states require everyone's consent: California, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Montana, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania and Washington. If your counselor is out of state, get their explicit agreement.
  • Decide where the audio goes before you press record. Keep it on your own device when you can. If an app sends it to the cloud, know which company and for how long.
  • Keep the focus on your ideas, not their voice. Re-listening for what was said is the point. Don't share the recording.

Between and after sessions

  • Take one line from each session into your week. A sentence you can come back to on a hard day.
  • Keep the thread going. Your therapist is one voice among several in your life: mentors, colleagues, the traditions you draw on. Hearing them together, regularly, helps the work last longer than six appointments.
